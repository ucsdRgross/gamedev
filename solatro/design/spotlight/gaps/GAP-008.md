# GAP-008 — `Q111`=(a)'s MECHANISM ("nearest") and its stated PURPOSE ("non-crossing") diverge on a column

- **Raised:** 2026-08-04, executing **S14**, from the owner's report while using the tuning tool:
  *"seeing beams cross over for no good reason in scenario s4 column section. last spotlight is not
  part of initial even distribution, assuming you added some cap that shouldnt exist according to my
  requirements."*
- **ANSWERED by the owner in chat, 2026-08-04 — none of (a)/(b)/(c); a FOURTH rule, and a better one:**

  > *"column beams with no crossing should be 100% possible. given column position, beam right above
  > it would point straight down to topmost card. Then alternating left and right from that center
  > beam, go down column. However we need it to scale to any set, such as multiple columns and rows
  > at same time. Going by the 1 column example, what needs to happen is that instead of choosing
  > closest, we first divide top bar into sections, getting wider by row. middle of top gets first
  > `-1-`, 2nd row gets lamp area surrounding first row `-212-`, and so on with `-32123-`, with each
  > lamp within a row section choosing its closest. This should reduce beam crossing as much as
  > possible."*

  **The allocation is driven by DEPTH, not by proximity.** Every option I offered argued about how to
  pair a flat list; the owner's rule changes what is being partitioned — the top bar is divided into
  bands that widen by row, and only WITHIN a band does "closest" apply.
- **Status: RESOLVED and implemented 2026-08-04; CORRECTED 2026-08-05 after the owner saw the first
  implementation was wrong for anything but a single column.**

  ⚠ **THE FIRST IMPLEMENTATION PARTITIONED THE BAR BY ROW AND THAT IS NOT WHAT THE RULE MEANS.** It
  gave the shallowest ROW the centre of the whole bar and deeper rows the outer bands — correct for
  one column, wrong for every other shape. The owner caught it on the six-card preset (three at depth
  0, three at depth 1, interleaved across six columns): *"beam separation looks wrong here"*.
  Measured: shallow cards were pulled to the middle and deep cards pushed to the edges, **inverting
  the x order — `col0 -> lamp2` while `col1 -> lamp0` — three crossings.**

  **The correct reading is TWO LEVELS, and the order of them is the whole thing:**
  1. **The bar divides into SECTIONS BY COLUMN, left to right.** A column with three lit cards gets
     three adjacent lamps; the next column right gets the next three. Sections are disjoint and in x
     order, so **no beam from one column can cross a beam from another.**
  2. **Inside a section, the fan is by DEPTH** — the topmost card takes the lamp nearest its own
     column (*"points straight down"*), each deeper card the next lamp outward.

  ⚠ **Ordered by DISTANCE, not by alternating left/right.** Strict alternation stops being monotone
  the moment a column is not centred in its own section — a column at the screen edge, or a ragged
  board — and monotone distance is the entire reason the fan cannot cross.

  ⚠ **WHY THE TEST DID NOT CATCH IT, AND THIS IS THE TRANSFERABLE PART: I tested the two shapes the
  RULE WAS DESCRIBED WITH — a column and a row — and not the shape that DISTINGUISHES the two
  readings.** Both readings agree on a pure column and on a pure row. Only an interleaved set
  separates them, and that was the one case with no test. **When implementing a rule from an example,
  the test that matters is the case the example does not cover.**

  |  rows present | bands | who gets what |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | `-1-` | the whole bar is that row's; its cards spread across it by x |
  | 2 | `-212-` | the shallower row takes the MIDDLE, the deeper one flanks it |
  | 3 | `-32123-` | each deeper row takes the next band out, on both sides |

  ⚠ **WHY IT CANNOT CROSS, which is the part worth keeping.** A deeper card's lamp is always further
  from the column than a shallower card's, and a lamp further out lands lower — so two beams on the
  same side converge on the target x without ever meeting, and two on opposite sides can only meet AT
  that x, which they reach at different heights. Greedy "nearest" has no such invariant.
  ⚠ **IT DEGRADES TO THE RIGHT THING FOR A ROW.** One depth means one band — the whole bar — so a row
  still pairs across the full width. The column behaviour is not a special case bolted on; it is the
  same rule with more depths. Both are asserted.
  ⚠ **"AS MUCH AS POSSIBLE", NOT "NEVER"** — the owner's own framing, and the honest bound. A set
  interleaving depths across the width can still cross, and that is accepted.
  **Verified:** `test_origins_assign_sorted_pairs_never_cross()` asserts, on a COLUMN, that lamp
  distance grows with depth and that **no pair of beams intersects** (real segment intersection, not
  an index pattern); on a ROW, that it still spreads full width with the leftmost card taking the
  leftmost lamp. SPOTLIGHT 90 → 93. Rendered and looked at: `04_S4.png` shows the fan — near-vertical
  onto the topmost card, each deeper one entering from further out, no crossings.
- **Severity:** **CONTRADICTION** (gap-protocol rule 3) — an answer whose chosen mechanism fails to
  produce the property the same answer gives as its reason.
- **Design nodes:** `Q111`=(a) · against chart **E2** (the assignment rule) and `Q111`'s own rationale.

## The two things that are not the same question

`Q111` — *"With several origins free, which does a new light take?"* · **(a)** *"nearest to its
target — **keeps beams mostly vertical and non-crossing**"*. This is **ORIGIN SELECTION** for one
light.

Chart **E2** — *"which leftover light goes to which leftover target"*, with **option A
(RECOMMENDED)**: *"sort both by target x; pair in order. Provably minimises beam crossings"*, option
B *"greedy nearest"*, option C Hungarian. This is **LIGHT→TARGET PAIRING** during a travel.
⚠ **Chart E2 has NO question gating it.** It is a chart-level recommendation that no answer ever
ratified, which is why nothing noticed the two rules were being conflated.

## The defect, measured

`SpotlightOrigins.take()` implemented `Q111`=(a) literally: for each light in turn, the free origin
whose x is nearest the target's. **On a ROW that gives what the answer promises.** On a **COLUMN**
every target shares one x, so "nearest" is a four-way tie and the greedy order resolves it worst:

```
4 targets at x=640, origins evenly at x = 160 / 480 / 800 / 1120
take #1 -> 480   (nearest)
take #2 -> 800   (next nearest)
take #3 -> 160
take #4 -> 1120  <- the FARTHEST remaining, and its beam crosses all three
```

That is the owner's *"last spotlight is not part of initial even distribution"* — it **is** one of the
four initial origins (there is no cap; `k0 = max(count, 4)`), but it is the worst one, so it reads as
an outlier. And it is the crossing.

⚠ **`Q111`=(a)'s own words are the argument against `Q111`=(a)'s mechanism here.** It chose "nearest"
*because* it "keeps beams mostly vertical and non-crossing". On a column the mechanism defeats the
purpose it was chosen for. Sorted pairing achieves the purpose in every case, including the row case
where the two rules already agree.

## What I did and why it needs your ruling

**Shipped:** `SpotlightOrigins.assign(targets)` — sorts targets by x, sorts free origins by x, pairs
in order — and both `SpotlightDirector` and the tuning tool now assign a whole section at once
through it. `take()` is kept for the genuinely single-light case (chart T's momentary cue, where
there is no set to sort) and still implements nearest.

**Why I did not park it:** the owner reported the crossing as a defect while looking at the tool, and
the fix serves the answer's stated purpose. **Why it is still a gap:** it deviates from the letter of
a recorded answer, and gap-protocol rule 2 says a choice that changes observable behaviour is not
mine to take silently. Verified by eye afterwards: the four beams now enter in left-to-right order
onto the column with no crossing.

## Options I offered — kept as the record of what I got wrong

⚠ **All three argued about how to PAIR a flat list, and the owner's answer changed what is being
PARTITIONED.** That is worth keeping: my options were a menu over one framing, and the framing was
the thing that needed replacing. `Q111` and chart E2 both frame this as "which lamp for which light";
neither asks "what shape should the rig make", which is the question a fan answers.



- **(a)** **Ratify what is shipped: sorted pairing for a SET, nearest for a single light.** `Q111`
  gains an option for it and chart E2's option A becomes the answer it never had. Consequence: the
  non-crossing property `Q111` promises actually holds, for rows and columns alike; one rule covers
  the travel pairing and the initial assignment, so E2 needs no separate mechanism. Cost: `Q111`=(a)'s
  literal wording changes from "nearest" to "sorted", and a single new light still uses nearest, so
  there are two rules to keep straight (they agree except on ties).
- **(b)** **Keep "nearest" everywhere and accept crossings on a column.** Consequence: no code
  change, the answer stands as written. But the owner has now reported the result as a defect, and a
  column is not a corner case — it is half of every act.
- **(c)** **Nearest, with ties broken by index order.** Consequence: the smallest possible change,
  and it fixes the *column* case specifically (equal x, so every pick is a tie). But it does nothing
  for a nearly-degenerate case — two columns one card apart — where the ties are approximate and
  greedy still inverts.
- *my recommendation* **(a)**. It is the only one where the property `Q111` was chosen FOR is the
  property the code has, and chart E2 already recommends exactly this rule for the sibling problem.
