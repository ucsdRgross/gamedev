## What the design says — four exemptions and one surface

Round 2 settled the structure: **`Q240`=(b), one full-screen screen-space surface**, with
`Q242`=(a) accepting that *"a beam crosses in front of (or behind) every prop it passes"*. §9's own
consequence list says it plainly: *"It is drawn over everything or under everything."*

Round 1 had already answered what the dim covers, one element at a time:

| Question | Answer | So this element must be… |
|---|---|---|
| `Q73` the **HUD** (buttons, score, deck/discard) | (a) **yes, dimmed** | BELOW the layer |
| `Q74` the **props** | (a) **no** — *"props are performers, they stay lit (PropLayer draws above the light layer)"* | ABOVE the layer |
| `Q75` the **score popups** | (a) **no**, the number stays readable | ABOVE the layer |
| `Q76` the **focus inspector panel** | (a) **no** | ABOVE the layer |
| `Q77` the **card glow** | (a) **yes** — this is the mechanism that makes the glow read only inside the circle and beam (G13) | BELOW the layer |

## What it does not say

**Where the one surface goes.** In this project's draw order — 100 % structural, `z_index == 0`
everywhere (`LAYERING.md`) — the HUD is a LATER sibling than everything on the board, while the
props, the score popups and the focus panel are all board children. A single surface above the HUD
is above all four exempt things; a single surface below the props is below the HUD too. **No
position satisfies the table.** `Q74`'s own parenthetical names the resolution it assumed —
*"PropLayer draws above the light layer"* — which `Q240`=(b) then made unavailable.

## Why it blocks

Rule 3, and rule 2 on top of it: the choices differ in what the player sees on every dim, and the
wrong one is expensive to reverse — it decides whether S13 is ONE node or two, which is a structural
change to `play_area.tscn` and to `LAYERING.md`'s contract, not a tuning value.

⚠ **This is `Q102`'s failure repeating.** §0b C1 caught *"in front of the card it is effecting, but
not in front of other objects in front of card"* against one surface, and `Q240`–`Q242` resolved it
by withdrawing `Q102`. The same withdrawal was never applied to `Q73`–`Q76`, which are the same
claim broken into four.

## Options I can see, in the questionnaire grammar

- **(a)** **One surface, above everything** — the literal `Q240`=(b). Props, score popups and the
  focus panel all dim with the board; `Q74`, `Q75` and `Q76` are withdrawn the way `Q102` was.
  Consequence: the cleanest code (one node, one pass) and the strongest effect, but a score popup
  read during a dim is dimmer than the same popup read outside one, and a prop mid-performance goes
  dark.
- **(b)** **Two surfaces: the DIM below the exempt things, the LIGHT above them.** The dim sits
  above the cards and below `PropLayer`/`OverlayLayer`; the circles and beams sit above everything.
  Consequence: every answer in the table is honoured and `Q242` still holds, at the cost of two
  passes over the screen and a second uniform upload — and the beam no longer punches its own hole
  in the dim in one operation, so H7's `dim * (1 - coverage)` has to be computed in both.
- **(c)** **One surface, above the board and below the HUD.** Cards and the card glow dim (`Q77`
  ✓), the HUD stays lit — `Q73` is withdrawn instead of `Q74`–`Q76`. Consequence: the props, the
  popups and the focus panel are all board children, so they dim anyway; only the HUD is saved. This
  honours the fewest answers of the three.
- *my recommendation* **(b)** — it is the only option that keeps every answer, and the exemptions
  are not incidental: `Q75` and `Q76` are both *"stays readable"*, which is a legibility
  requirement rather than a look preference, and `Q74` calls props *"performers"*.
