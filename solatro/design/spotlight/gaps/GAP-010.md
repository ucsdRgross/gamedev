# GAP-010 — does an `act_event_cap` trip VOID the section's score, or bank what stands?

- **Raised:** 2026-08-06, by the post-build code review (finding: "overrun act still banks
  half-resolved score").
- **Status: OPEN — behaviour unchanged pending the owner's answer.**

## The gap

`_spotlight_section` aborts its sweep on `act_cancelled or act_overrun` (`game.gd`), but its caller
`score_line` re-checks only `act_cancelled` before re-scoring and banking. So when the runaway guard
(`act_event_cap`, gate G1.6) trips mid-sweep, the section is left **half-resolved** — some hooks
fired, some compactions landed, some never ran — and `score_line` then re-derives a hand from that
board and banks it via `add_line_score`. The remaining lines of the act keep scoring too.

The design ratifies neither reading:

- **(a) Bank what stands.** Consistent with the cap's existing behaviour elsewhere: `run_props`
  breaks its loop on overrun but the act still completes and applies its score. The cap is a brake on
  *processing*, not an act-voider — `Q201`=(b) rejected per-section caps precisely because the
  act-level guard was to be the only bound.
- **(b) Void the line (treat like `act_cancelled`).** The guard just declared the state a runaway;
  banking a score derived from it commits a number no completed rules pass produced, and a player
  who builds a deliberate near-cap engine gets a different payout depending on where the cap lands.

The asymmetry between the two flags is real either way: today `act_cancelled` skips banking and
`act_overrun` does not, and no comment says that split is chosen rather than accidental.

### Answer

- **(a)** bank what stands (current behaviour; document it at the `score_line` guard)
- **(b)** void the line: `if act_cancelled or act_overrun: return` after `_spotlight_section`
- **(c)** other — describe.
