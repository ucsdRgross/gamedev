## What the design says — twice, incompatibly

**Chart O11** (v2, written before round 1's answers landed):

> radial COLOUR SHIFT: core to mid to edge. This is a **`PaletteRamp` SAMPLE** on intensity, exactly
> as fire samples `u_ramp` — *the palette contract forbids lerping colours, and a ramp is the fix,
> not a limit*

§16's glow table types it the same way: `glow_ramp` — *"the core→mid→edge `PaletteRamp`, sampled on
intensity"*, and `PLAN.md` §1.8 copies that typing into a normative contract.

**Round 1's answers**, folded into `DESIGN.md` §0b at the "remaining 16" list:

> **`Q214`=(a) dither the bands — but the gradient is NOT forced onto the fixed palette**
> (consistent with `Q134`=b, light gets off-palette freedom)

`Q134`=(b) is *"light gets freedom to use off-palette colour from the start"*; `Q135`=(b) is
*"an off-palette exception for the light layer only"*; `Q214`'s free-text note is *"gradient
shouldnt be forced to be on fixed palette"*.

## What it does not say

**Which resource type `FxGlowStyle.glow_ramp` is** — and the two candidates cannot both be it.
`Scripts/palette_ramp.gd` is an `Array[int]` of palette indices resolved against
`PaletteDB.PALETTE`, and it *never* interpolates (that is the whole point of the class, stated in
its header). So a `PaletteRamp` glow can emit **only** palette entries, which is exactly what
`Q134`=(b) and `Q214`'s note say the light is exempt from.

## Why it blocks

Rule 3 (a self-contradiction) and rule 2 (it is an owner call about the look, and it is expensive to
reverse: the type of this export decides the shader's colour path in S12, the contents of all three
`.tres`, and whether a new resource class exists at all).

It is **not** resolvable by recency the way `Q84`/`Q168` was: O11 is a chart node the owner
confirmed, and the answers are the owner's own words. Nothing distinguishes them but which one is
believed to have been superseded.

## Options I can see, in the questionnaire grammar

- **(a)** `glow_ramp` is a **`PaletteRamp`**, as O11 and §16 say. Every glow colour is a palette
  entry; the `dither` knob (`Q214`=a) breaks the band edges, and additive blending over card art
  produces off-palette pixels anyway (`Q138`=a). Consequence: the glow can never be brighter or
  more saturated than the palette's brightest entry, and a core→mid→edge shift costs three entries
  the palette must already contain.
- **(b)** `glow_ramp` is a **`Gradient`** (Godot's built-in) or a small new off-palette ramp
  resource — the light layer's exception `Q135`=(b) grants. Consequence: light is the one thing in
  the game that is not palette-bound; the "every colour is a palette entry" suite needs an explicit
  carve-out for it, and the palette contract stops being universal by construction.
- **(c)** `PaletteRamp` **plus a brightness/tint escape** — palette entries for the hue, an
  off-palette multiplier for the core. Consequence: keeps the entries recognisable but reintroduces
  the lerp O11 rejected, at the hot end only.
- *my recommendation* **(b)** — `Q134`, `Q135` and `Q214` are three separate answers all saying
  off-palette, and O11's justification (*"the palette contract forbids lerping colours"*) is the
  claim those three answers overturned. But it is a look call and I will not take it.
