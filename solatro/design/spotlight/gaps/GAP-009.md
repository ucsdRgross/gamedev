# GAP-009 — what SIZES the row opening: the LIFTED cards, the LIT cards, or the larger of the two?

- **Raised:** 2026-08-05, starting **S16** (derived row expansion).
- **FIRST ANSWER, 2026-08-05:** *"it should be by the lowest card."*
- ⚠ **SUPERSEDED THE SAME DAY — THE OWNER CHANGED THEIR MIND, AND THE REASON RETIRES THE WHOLE DERIVED
  MODEL:**

  > *"If it was actually lowest, then bottom rows would rise up if score was something like flush and
  > every single card jumped, but that would cause shifting in cards that arent part of actual scored
  > set. Instead allow choosing between card height of the row, so that total separation becomes card
  > height, or choosing by cards that jump so that cards that dont jump are slightly covered, which
  > would be card height - jump height."*

  **The counter-example kills "lowest".** On a flush every card in the row is lit and jumps, so the
  lowest lit card is the lowest card on the board — and sizing from it lifts rows that have nothing to
  do with the scored set. A rule meant to uncover one buried card moved the whole board.

- **Status: RESOLVED — the opening is a CHOICE BETWEEN TWO FIXED FORMULAS, not a derivation.**
  **K10b's "no visible gap, sized from the lowest ___" is RETIRED** — both of these are computed from
  the card metrics, and neither looks at which card is lowest:

  | mode | separation | what it looks like |
  |---|---|---|
  | **`CARD_HEIGHT`** | one full card height | the row opens completely; nothing stays covered |
  | **`JUMP_ADJUSTED`** | card height − jump height | a card that does NOT jump stays slightly covered |

  ⚠ **IT IS A KNOB, NOT A RULING — "allow choosing between" is the answer itself.** Ships as
  `spotlight_separation_mode` in `player_settings.gd` so the owner picks by eye in the tuning tool.
  ⚠ `Q43`=(a) (a fixed card height) is **no longer superseded**; it is now the `CARD_HEIGHT` mode.
  ⚠ K10c still stands — the duck is computed ONCE and does not track card bottoms afterwards.

### Superseded, kept only to explain why the derived model is gone

  **Which of (a)/(b)/(c) that is, and why it does not matter:** `Q265`=(c) makes the lit set a
  SUPERSET of the jumping set, so the lowest lit card is never higher than the lowest lifted one.
  **(b) and (c) therefore describe the same card and the same opening**, and the answer selects both;
  only **(a)** — K10b's literal "lowest LIFTED" — is excluded. So:

  > **K10b is CORRECTED: the opening is sized from the lowest card in the row that must be visible —
  > i.e. the lowest LIT card — not from the lowest lifted one.** A buried lit non-meld card is
  > uncovered, which is the purpose K10d names for the reveal.

  ⚠ Consequence to carry into S16, and it is `Q52`'s own warning: because every lit card now sizes its
  row, **a long column expands nearly every row on the board** and by more than the meld alone would
  need. That is the accepted cost of the answer, not a bug to tune away.
  ⚠ Unchanged by this: K10c still holds — the duck is **computed once** at the start and does not
  track card bottoms afterwards, so a card moving later may open a gap and that is accepted (the
  owner's own note on `Q265`: *"gap is allowed if all cards jump at same time since once ducking has
  completed it shouldnt track current card bottoms"*).

## The gap

`DESIGN.md` §chart K states the sizing rule **twice, and the two do not agree**:

- **K10b** (v6, presented as settled): *"The covering cards duck by exactly enough that there is NO
  VISIBLE GAP between the top of a ducking card and the top of a lifted card, **sized from the LOWEST
  LIFTED card in that row**."*
- **K10d**, immediately after it, marked **🔶 FOR REVIEW**: *"under `Q265`=c only the MELD lifts, but
  the reveal exists to uncover a buried LIT card, and a lit non-meld card never lifts. **Is the opening
  sized by the lifting cards, the lit cards, or the larger of the two?**"*

`Q265`=(c) is answered — *"no — keep today's behaviour, only the best hand jumps, and the lit set is
simply wider than the jumping set"* — but it answers **which cards JUMP**, not **which cards SIZE THE
OPENING**. K10d is the question it leaves standing, and it is still flagged for review.

⚠ **This is the stream's recurring defect shape exactly** (see
`.claude/memory/seam-checks-not-rereading.md`): two representations of one fact, with nothing
comparing them. K10b reads as decided and would be implemented without ever reading K10d.

## Why it cannot be taken under gap-protocol rule 1

Rule 1 covers what is reversible **and clearly within intent**. This is neither:

- **The two readings disagree on a COMMON case, not an edge case.** `Q265`=(c) guarantees the lit set
  is *wider* than the jumping set, so a row holding a lit non-meld card is the normal shape of a
  scored row, not a corner.
- **The separating input:** a buried, lit, non-meld card in a row where the meld cards sit higher.
  Sized by **lifted**, the opening clears the meld and that card **stays covered** — the reveal fails
  at the purpose K10d names for it. Sized by **lit** (or the larger), it is uncovered, and every row
  containing any lit card opens further than the meld alone requires, which under `Q52` (*"Column
  scoring on the longest column expands nearly every row at once"*) is a much larger board movement.
- It is not a knob; it changes what the player sees on essentially every submit.

## What the owner needs to choose

- **(a) the LIFTED cards** — K10b as literally written. Smallest movement; a buried lit non-meld card
  can stay covered, and the reveal does not do the job K10d says it exists for.
- **(b) the LIT cards** — the reveal always uncovers everything that is lit, which is the stated
  purpose. Largest movement, and under `Q52` it is close to a whole-board expansion on a long column.
- **(c) the LARGER of the two** — never smaller than the meld needs, never leaves a lit card buried.
  The safe reading, and the most board movement of the three.
