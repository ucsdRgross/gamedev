### Why (a) was withdrawn — MEASURED, and confirmed by eye

The wall camera does not pan inside the board's scene. Chain:
`Wall` → `%Camera2D` → `%Pictures` → `WallPicture` → `%Screen` (a **`Sprite2D` whose texture is the
SubViewport's `ViewportTexture`**), while the `SubViewport` hosting `GameView`/`PlayArea` hangs
separately under `%Viewports`. **So the camera frames a flat pre-rendered texture.** Today's
`board_zoom` re-renders the board INTO that texture at a larger scale; a camera can only MAGNIFY it.

```
board_zoom (focused, 3-grid board)   1.940559
viewport.size                        (3656, 685) -- IDENTICAL in both modes
today's chain  screen.scale * camera.zoom   0.964905 screen px per texture px
simulated (a)  same chain x board_zoom      1.8725   screen px per texture px
```

**By eye, same probe run, the same five dealt cards:** today's render has legible rank glyphs
(`5`, `8`, `Q`, `2`, `10`) and clean suit pips; the simulated-(a) render has the same cards visibly
softer — mushy pips, lost edges. **Real, eye-visible quality loss on pixel art, not a rounding
artefact.**

⚠ **THE OBVIOUS ESCAPE IS CLOSED.** Rendering the picture larger to buy texels hits `S31`'s
`game_picture_max_render_px` clamp: `3656 x 1.94 ≈ 7092`, against a 4096 cap.
⚠ `WallPicture.update_filter()` uses `NEAREST` at rest and `LINEAR` only while the camera zoom is
CHANGING, so the measured degradation is nearest-neighbour detail loss; a live zoom under (a) would
be bilinear — smoother blur rather than blockiness, likely worse. Unmeasured.

## What was found

`PlayArea` lives inside a `SubViewport` whose size is fixed for the session at
`game_picture_design_size` — `grid_max_count` grid positions at OVERVIEW scale.

In FOCUSED mode `focused_board_zoom()` scales `scroll_container` UP so the grid's block fills the
window, while `_apply_board_zoom_rect()` deliberately pins the scroller's on-screen rect to the
UNZOOMED window size (`offset_right`/`offset_bottom` divided by `board_zoom`). **The scroller then
CLIPS the enlarged content to that fixed window, and `pan_to_grid()`'s `scroll_x_to` selects which
slice is visible.**

⚠ **Content scrolled outside that window is never rendered into the SubViewport texture at all — it
is clipped away, not merely off-camera.** So the texture already IS the cropped single-grid slice.

**A camera can only crop pixels that exist.** Moving the horizontal pan to a camera offset requires
the texture to hold the full unclipped board at the target scale — the opposite of what the pipeline
produces. This is why `TP-105` could not simply be a re-point, and why the step was reported
`blocked` rather than guessed at.

## Options as put

- **(a) ANSWERED — move the focused zoom onto the camera.** Above.
- **(b) Camera steps in overview only; the scroller pans when focused.** Matches `H23`'s literal
  split and needs no rewrite: focused content genuinely exceeds the window so the scroller has real
  range, and in overview the texture holds the whole board. Cheapest. Costs: `H22` stepping exists
  only in overview, and two mechanisms remain.
- **(c) Grow the SubViewport to hold the focused spread.** Violates the fixed-render-target rule
  (*"one fixed size for the whole session, nothing resizes a render target mid-show"*) and at ~3x the
  current 3656 px would blow the 4096 GPU clamp outright. Not recommended, not chosen.
