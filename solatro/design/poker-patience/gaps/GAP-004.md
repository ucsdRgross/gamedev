# GAP-004 — a non-square grid's DIAG length is undecided

status: resolved
outcome: answered
raised: 2026-08-26, during execution plan step S6 (line enumeration)
design: PLAN.md §1.4 (`Q96`, `Q97`, `Q98`); DESIGN.md §10 (`Q96`-`Q98`); GAP-001 (grids are
  not guaranteed 5x5, and may be non-square)
severity: GAP

## OWNER ANSWER — a diagonal is a SAME-RATE line; corners are not part of the definition

Owner, verbatim:

> *"diagonals are just lines where x and y increase or decreases at same rate. corners dont
> matter."*

The rule this produces:

- A diagonal is any run whose **x and y change at the same rate** — `|dx| == |dy|`. Where it
  starts and ends is irrelevant; **"corner-to-corner" was never the definition**, only a
  description of what that happens to look like on a square grid.
- So a **non-square grid is no longer ambiguous**. The corner requirement was the only thing
  that had no answer there, and it is not a requirement.
- A diagonal's full length is bounded by the smaller of the grid's two dimensions:
  `min(grid_width, grid_height)`. Only full-length lines score, unchanged.
- On a square grid this yields **exactly the two long diagonals**, which is what the earlier
  answer described in the first place — the two readings agree wherever the earlier one was
  defined, and this one is defined everywhere.

**The scope of this answer, confirmed by the owner.** Asked whether "x and y change at the
same rate" also retracts the four *one-horizontal-axis-plus-height* climbs — `(±1, 0, +1)` and
`(0, ±1, +1)`, where only one of x and y moves — the owner confirmed it does not:

> *"yeah the height cases are fine, i was making example of the height being same cases since
> it was about the grid at zero height."*

So this answer governs the FLAT, zero-height case that GAP-004 asked about. The climbing
family stays exactly as `Q86` chose it: the four one-axis climbs and the four corner-to-corner
climbs, eight in all.


## What the design says

`Q97`=a: "only full-length lines score, so in a 5x5 that is the two long ones." `Q96`=a: both
corner-to-corner diagonals of a grid score. `Q98`=a: no wrapping. All three answers are framed
against a square (5x5, or "n x n") grid, where "corner-to-corner" and "full-length" agree on
one answer: a run of `n` cells from one corner to the opposite one, `dz` fixed and `dx`/`dy`
both stepping every cell.

`Q86`=c extends the same corner-to-corner shape into height: the four `(dx=±1, dy=±1, dz=+1)`
3-D climbs are corner-to-corner in exactly the same sense.

## What the repo says

GAP-001's answer confirms grids are not guaranteed square — 6x6 and non-square shapes are
explicitly expected. `LineGeometry._diagonals()` (S6) only builds a corner-to-corner DIAG
line (flat or climbing) when `grid_width == grid_height`, and skips that direction entirely
otherwise.

## What it does not say

On a non-square grid (say 4 wide, 6 tall), a "corner-to-corner" run has no single answer:
- Walking every column (length 4) leaves 2 rows short of the far corner.
- Walking every row (length 6) runs off the grid's left/right edge before reaching it.
- Neither reading is "full-length" in both axes at once, so `Q97`'s "only full-length lines
  score" does not resolve which axis's length wins, or whether corner-to-corner diagonals
  simply do not exist on a non-square grid.

## Why it matters

S6 (line enumeration) and later S9 (placement scoring) need one answer per direction to build
each DIAG line; guessing either reading silently picks a length nobody confirmed, and finds
(or fails to find) lines a player looking at a non-square grid would reasonably expect.

## What I did meanwhile

`LineGeometry` skips every corner-to-corner DIAG direction (flat and the four 3-D climbs)
when `grid_width != grid_height`. The axis-aligned climbs — `(±1, 0, +1)` and `(0, ±1, +1)` —
are unaffected: each moves along only one spatial axis, so its length is that axis's own size,
same as ROW/COL, with no ambiguity. The square case (the only shape any fixture uses) is
fully implemented and tested.

## Options I can see

- **(a)** the SHORTER axis wins — length = `min(grid_width, grid_height)`, anchored to one
  corner, so the run reaches one pair of opposite corners exactly and stops short of the
  other two · simplest, but "corner-to-corner" becomes true for only one of the two flat
  diagonals' corners
- **(b)** the LONGER axis wins — length = `max(grid_width, grid_height)`, and the shorter axis
  clamps/repeats at its own edge · keeps both diagonals full-length in the dominant axis, but
  needs its own new rule for what the clamped axis does at the boundary
- **(c)** no corner-to-corner DIAG line exists at all on a non-square grid — only the
  axis-aligned climbs remain · consistent with "corner-to-corner" requiring equal axes, but
  removes flat-diagonal scoring (and half of Q86's 3-D family) from every non-square grid
- **(d)** a non-square grid gets its own bounded 5x5 sub-square's worth of diagonals per
  design-owner call, decided per shape rather than by a formula · most flexible, most work,
  and re-opens the "what counts as full-length" question per grid
