status: OPEN — PARKED FOR THE OWNER

⚠⚠ **THE OWNER DID NOT RULE ON THIS.** They stepped away with a standing instruction — *"if a gap
shows up, take recommended for now."* Option (a) was taken. **Review before building further.**

## ⚠⚠ PARKED — (a) WAS ATTEMPTED AND MEASURED WRONG. THIS NEEDS AN OWNER RULING.

**Do not take a "recommended" option here.** Answering it means choosing the zoom number that decides
how the game looks at its most-used view, and `H5`'s word *"readable"* is exactly what no default can
supply. The standing authorisation to take recommended options does not stretch this far.

### What was measured when (a) was attempted

`grid_state()` **reproduces `resting_state()` exactly** when `grid_index == resting_grid` (its own
docstring says so). So swapping the OVERVIEW branch to `resting_state()` changes nothing: same
`focused_scale()` fill-and-crop, `zoom 0.964905`, still ~`1194` px of a `3656` px picture, still one
grid — confirmed by render and by eye, then reverted. **No existing pose function expresses "fit the
whole design width"**; `resting_state`, `panned_state`, `grid_state` and `focused_scale` all fill to
window height and crop to aspect.

### And fitting the whole picture is NOT the answer either

```
zoom to fit 3656 px into an 1152 px window   = 0.315
a 216 px cell block then renders at          ~68 px on screen
                                             five columns in 68 px
```
That is a thumbnail, not an overview. **`H5` says "as many grids as fit at a READABLE zoom, and
pans"** — and `Q180`/`Q181` already rejected the squashed-whole-board reading once.

⚠ **THE REAL ODDITY UNDERNEATH, and it may be the actual question:** a grid position is `1219` px
wide while a grid's cell block is only `216` px — roughly a `1000` px gap of empty board between
neighbours. That is why three positions cannot fit readably. It traces to the standing unruled item:
**`grid_buffer_px` is 220 RAW px against a 216 px block**, with §1i's table stated at `card_scale`
2.5 while the game ships at 1.0. The owner has twice declined to rule on it. **Ruling on the overview
zoom without ruling on that may be fixing the symptom.**

## Options as they now stand

- **(a) Fit the whole picture.** Measured: unreadable at `~68` px per cell block. **Withdrawn.**
- **(b) Accept a one-grid overview.** Costs `H5` and `H6`; the two modes then differ only in content
  scale. Cheapest, and it is what ships today.
- **(d) NEW — an overview zoom that shows as many grids as fit AT A READABLE FLOOR, and pans for the
  rest.** This is `H5` read literally, and it **keeps `H22`'s stepping meaningful** rather than
  emptying it the way (a) would. Needs a readability floor — a minimum on-screen size for a cell
  block — which is a tunable the owner must set, not a default anyone can infer.
- **(e) NEW — close the position/content gap first** (`grid_buffer_px`, `card_scale`), so grids sit
  nearer together and more than one fits readably without any new zoom at all. Addresses the cause
  rather than the symptom, and would need no `H5` compromise.

## SUPERSEDED — the original (a) answer



**The overview zooms the CAMERA out to frame the whole picture — every grid position at once.
Focusing zooms it back in to one position.** That restores `H5` and makes the two modes mean
different things again.

⚠ **AND IT IS BLUR-SAFE, which is why it survives `GAP-024`'s objection.** `GAP-024` rejected moving
the FOCUSED zoom to the camera because that MAGNIFIES a fixed `3656x685` texture by `1.94` and
visibly softens the card art. **Zooming OUT MINIFIES** — the opposite direction — so the overview
loses no sharpness. The focused mode keeps the scroller's crisp re-render exactly as `GAP-024`=(b)
ruled. The two answers compose.

## What was found — MEASURED

After `GAP-024`=(b) the camera's zoom is the same in both modes; only `board_zoom` (the scroller's
content scale) differs, `1.0` overview vs `1.940559` focused. So both modes frame the same ~1194 px
of picture. With `S31e` spreading grids one per `1219` px position (cell blocks at `504 / 1723 /
2942`), **a neighbouring grid is never inside the camera's rect.**

Three checks report it, each from a different angle:
```
TP-140 precondition: in the OVERVIEW a neighbouring grid is in frame beside the middle one
                     -- grid 0 false grid 2 false
TP-106: the grid the view rests on is wholly in frame before panning -- [0] in frame, resting on 1
TP-101 precondition: three grids are wider than the window -- content 3640.000000 window 3656.000000
```
⚠ **`TP-140`'s precondition is the instrument-proving one** — it exists to show the test can SEE a
neighbour before asserting that focusing removes it. It is not calibration noise; it is the check
telling us the overview has stopped being an overview.

## Why this is a gap and not a defect

`H5` (*"as many grids as fit at a readable zoom"*) and `H22` (*"the picture frame holds 3 grid
positions; the camera steps between them"*) were both satisfiable while grids were packed adjacent
INSIDE one position. `GAP-021` spread them one per position, and `GAP-024`=(b) made the camera the
overview's view. Neither ruling noticed it left `H5` unsatisfiable. **No answered node says what the
overview's zoom should be**, and two defensible answers differ in observable behaviour.

## Options

- **(a) TAKEN — the camera zooms out in the overview.** Frames the whole picture, all grid positions
  visible; focusing zooms back to one. Restores `H5` and `H6` (*"all-grids is orientation: clicking
  a grid zooms in on it"*), which is meaningless if only one grid is ever shown. Minification, so no
  blur. ⚠ Costs: `H22`'s STEPPING becomes a focused-mode motion rather than an overview one, since a
  fully-zoomed-out overview has nothing to step across — `TP-105` must be re-checked against that.
- **(b) Accept a one-grid overview.** The modes differ only in content scale. Cheapest — nothing to
  build. Costs `H5`, `H6` and the point of having two modes at all.
- **(c) Re-pack the grids adjacent again**, so several fit inside one camera window. Undoes
  `GAP-021`, which the owner ruled deliberately. Rejected.
