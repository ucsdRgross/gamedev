# GAP-038 — "the HUD scales with the picture" cannot hold while the board lays out in picture pixels

status: open
raised: the geometry pass (SceneRoot scale + PlayContainer height + HUD offset + focused framing)
design: `DESIGN.md` §36 (chart H), `PLAN.md` §1.2 (centred layout), §1.13 (`H1`, `Q166`, `Q168`)
severity: THE RULINGS CONTRADICT THE CODE — highest priority under the gap protocol's case 3

## What the rulings say

> *"HUD scales with the picture"* — `SceneRoot` keeps its authored `1152x648` and the whole canvas
> scales uniformly to the picture (`1495x841`, ratio 1.2977).

> *"the grid should be offset from how much space the hud rectangle takes."*

> *"clicking on grid zooms in but everything is clipped instead of fitting in 5x5 grid + entrance
> row as minimum size."*

## Why they cannot all hold as written — MEASURED, not inferred

**The board lays out DIRECTLY IN PICTURE PIXELS today.** Measured through
`Tests/Visual/focused_pose_probe` on the product's own path: `play_area.size` is `(1495, 841)`, the
picture's design size exactly, and the cell block is `216x286` at `board_zoom` 1.0. Every board
quantity — `grid_block_size_px`, `isolating_grid_buffer_px`, `grid_position_size_px` — is in those
same units, and `game_picture_design_size()` IS their span.

So scaling `SceneRoot` by 1.2977 makes the board's own 1495-wide span render at **1940 px inside a
1495 px picture**. The picture stops fitting the board it is sized for.

**And the height identity is load-bearing.** `focused_board_zoom()` is `size.y / (block_h + strip)`
while `isolating_grid_buffer_px()` solves isolation at `picture.y / (block_h + strip)`. Those agree
only because `PlayContainer`'s height tracks the picture. Returning it to its authored 636 was
measured: `GRID VIEW` goes from 1 failure to 3, with both halves of `TP-140` red — grid 0 and grid 2
both in frame while another grid is focused, intruding ~196 px each. The derived buffer sits
EXACTLY on the isolation boundary (`2.2916 x (211.75 + 108) = 732.7` against a visible half-width of
`732.8`), so any reduction in the focused zoom breaks it.

**Offsetting the board by the HUD's rectangle moves the grid off centre.** The HUD's bounding rect is
`x 34..402, y 18..586` (measured). Insetting the board's window by 402 shifts the board's centre
right by 201, so a focused grid no longer sits at the picture's centre and the LEFT neighbour moves
201 px further into frame. That contradicts §1.2's centred layout, `TP-101`'s "every pan lands a
grid centred", and the isolation derivation at once.

## Options

- **(a)** The picture is the AUTHORED CANVAS scaled up. `game_picture_design_size()` becomes the
  authored `1152x648` (or a fixed multiple of it), the board's span is derived in authored units and
  must FIT that canvas, and `SceneRoot` no longer scales at all — the picture's own render size is
  what grows. The HUD then scales with the picture for free, because everything does.
- **(b)** Only the HUD scales. `SceneRoot` keeps filling the picture; the furniture controls get
  their own scale factor `design / authored` applied to position and size, so the composition is
  preserved proportionally while the board keeps laying out in picture pixels. Nothing about the
  board's geometry, the buffer derivation or `TP-139`/`TP-140`/`TP-141` moves.
- **(c)** The board keeps the full picture width and the HUD stops overlapping it by being moved
  rather than by shrinking the board — the furniture is placed in the picture's own left margin,
  which the buffer already reserves, instead of the board's area being reduced.
- **(d)** The HUD is not in the picture at all. The furniture moves to the wall overlay, outside the
  `SubViewport`, so it scales with the WINDOW rather than the picture and never covers the board.

## What any answer must not break

- `TP-139` (the focused grid is as tall as its window), `TP-140` (focusing takes the other grids out
  of the window) and `TP-141` (a non-focused grid paints nothing outside the board window). All
  three are green today and all three read the identity between the play area's height and the
  picture's.
- §1.2's centred layout and `TP-101`.
- `game_picture_design_size()` staying ONE fixed size for every run (`H1`): a deck that unlocks a
  second grid mid-show must not resize a render target.

## The separable half — NOT blocked by this

The focused view's clipping has a second, independent cause that any of the four options leaves in
place: `WallPicture.focused_scale()` applies `wall_overfill_margin` (1.02) to a picture that already
matches the window's aspect, because `game_picture_design_size()` rounds the height to a whole pixel
and the two axis ratios then differ by 6e-5 — enough for `is_equal_approx` to read them as unequal.
Measured cost: the camera shows `1465.69 x 824.45` of a `1495 x 841` picture, cutting **8.28 px off
the top and bottom** and 14.66 px off each side. The focused cell block's top is cut by 5.28 px,
which is the owner-reported clipped top row.

## ⚠ OWNER RULING — centred means centred in what the HUD LEAVES

> *"assuming that hud is a rectangle taking up a portion of the side of the screen, center of screen
> for stuff like grid should be center of remaining space not taken by the hud."*

This settles the objection this gap raised against ruling (c): the grid moving off the PICTURE's
centre is not a contradiction of §1.2, because **centred was never about the picture — it is about
the space the player can actually use.** §1.2's centring and `TP-101` should both be read against
the post-HUD rect.

⚠ **NOT YET BUILT.** It moves the board's window and therefore its centre, which changes where a
focused grid's neighbours fall — the same quantity `GAP-039` is about. Do the two together, and
measure `TP-140` and `TP-101` against the post-HUD rect rather than the play area's.

⚠ The HUD's own rectangle is measured: `x 34..402, y 18..586` in the picture's units, so it costs
the board 402 px of width on the left and nothing on the right.

### The centring ruling is BUILT, and it costs two more checks

`GameView` publishes the HUD's authored right edge (402) as `PlayArea.board_inset_left`; the board
**insets its own scroller** rather than offsetting its content, so every centring the board already
does — the focused aim, the resting position, the removal re-centre — lands in the post-HUD space
for free. Measured at 1147x649: post-HUD centre 774.5, cell-block centre 783.8, off by **9.3 px**,
which is the panel's asymmetric score gutters and not a centring error (`TP-103` documents it).

⚠ **`hud_reserve_px()` CAPS THE RESERVE SO A GRID STILL FITS.** The HUD is authored against a
1152-wide canvas and does not shrink: on a 412 px phone its 402 px rectangle left the board **ten
pixels**, with the whole grid off screen. The cap is derived from the panel the board must show —
"a grid still fits" — never a fraction or an authored floor. It keeps the board legible; it does
NOT stop the HUD overlapping it. **That is what this gap still has to answer.**

⚠ **THE RESERVE ARRIVES AFTER THE SHOW HAS OPENED.** `_capture_furniture_authored_x()` runs at the
END of `GameView._ready()`, by which time the deal has built the board and a focused grid has been
fitted against an inset of zero. The setter re-applies the rect UNCONDITIONALLY and then re-focuses:
`_zoom_board_to()` early-returns when the zoom has not moved, which is exactly the case a reserve
that only changes the WIDTH produces, and the board then keeps the offsets it took when it thought
it had the whole screen.

**Cost, measured:** shifting the board right by half the reserve pushes the left neighbour further
into frame. `TP-141` (a non-focused grid paints nothing outside the board window) now fails at
29458 px changed, and a second pan check joins `TP-101`. Full suite 7 -> 9 failures. This is the
same quantity `GAP-039` turns on, and neither can be closed without the other.
