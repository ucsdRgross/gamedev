Combolands Buildings: What Sources Confirm
Quick Answer
Crux Games advertises 120+ buildings. This page lists the
45 that are named in a public source — a patch note, a Steam
 Community guide, or a discussion thread — together with what that source actually says. It is
 not the full roster, and it does not pretend to be. For the complete list in game, v1.0.5 added
 an in-game Compendium.
Crux Games advertises 120+ buildings. This table is not all of them — it is every building named in a source you can open yourself: the developers' patch notes and announcements, the community guides on the Steam hub, and the launch-week discussion threads. Where a source gives a number we quote the number and say which patch it came from; where it does not, the effect column says what the source actually claimed and nothing more. Nothing here is invented to fill a row.
Version note: Numbers marked (demo) come from Next Fest demo patch notes in April–June 2026, before the 1.0 progression overhaul. Treat them as historical unless a 1.0 patch note repeats them.

## How buildings are described in game
The Japanese-language community guide breaks a card down into four parts, which is the clearest
 public explanation of what you are reading when you hover a building: its guild and rarity, its
 activation condition, its effect — where a light-blue targeting line means adjacent and
 an orange line means range — plus the terrain it can be placed on, and its tags.
Tags are what most of the cross-building effects actually key off. Sunflower Field reads the
crop tag while Scarecrow reads farm; Ranch reads husbandry; and a
 launch-week thread notes that adding the irrigator tag to scoring buildings is one of the
 few cases where a multiplier on a non-scoring building matters. More on that on the
scoring page.
## Blueprints
Blueprints let you re-place a building you have already seen, and they stack — a v1.0.6 fix
 addressed duplicate blueprints failing to stack, and a v1.0.4 fix addressed duplicates always
 arriving with only one charge. You can hold a maximum of three, and unlike buildings they do not
 advance the week when used. One of the demo-era potions hands you a
 blueprint of a target building outright.

Combolands Milestones: Targets, Weeks & When to Bank
Quick Answer
Each Combolands milestone is a score target plus a number of weeks. Once you unlock the neutral option to end a milestone the moment you hit its target, the real decision each milestone becomes whether to bank early or spend the remaining weeks compounding your engine.
## The clock and the target
Every milestone gives you two numbers: a score target and the weeks remaining to reach it.
Placing a building advances the week; blueprints do not, which is part of why they are
worth holding. The Steam store screenshots show the early rungs — Small Town with a 3,000 target,
Capital City with 13,000 — so the ramp is steep from the start.
There is also an extension time shown alongside the target: even past the intended period you can
keep going up to that limit. Sitting between those two numbers is the whole tempo question.
## Bank early or keep building?
Once you have the neutral unlock that lets you close a milestone the instant you hit its target — it
needs Neutral level 7 and a finished run — every milestone poses the same question. Ending early
banks the win and moves you on. Playing out the remaining weeks does nothing for this target but
compounds the engine you carry into the next one, which is always bigger.
The bias in a compounding game is toward staying. The endless-mode reports make the
maths visible: once a build doubles its own weekly output faster than the target doubles, the gap
never closes again. But there is no in-game guidance on when the trade flips, and it is one of the ten
things a launch-week guide video lists as under-explained.
## Council requests are milestone-scoped
Council requests are set against the current milestone, not the run — the Spymaster’s request is
to collect 75 gold during a milestone, and screenshots show requests phrased as “transform 6 more
buildings this milestone”. Two consequences:
A request you cannot finish this milestone is dead weight, so read the scope before accepting.
Since v1.0.6 your first Council Request refresh is free, so there is no cost to rerolling one
bad offer. Further refreshes cost gold and get more expensive each time.
Detail on the councillors page.
## Past the named milestones
Clear all ten ranks and the named milestones give way to Megalopolis I, II, III and upward. That
is endless mode, where the target doubles roughly every eight weeks while a good
build doubles every week. Players have taken it far enough to break the score requirements at
Megalopolis XXXV.
## Where the difficulty actually is
Steam’s public stats show the milestone structure is hardest at the beginning, not the end: 53.9% of
owners have cleared the Villager rank and 40.5% have cleared Yeoman — a 13.4-point drop, larger than
any gap further up the rank ladder. If you are losing milestones, the fix is usually in
trigger chaining rather than in tempo decisions.

Combolands Tips: 10 Things Worth Knowing Early
Quick Answer
The tips that change results fastest in Combolands: read targeting-line colour before placement value, treat removals as a tool rather than a mistake, use the Merchant to trade 3 gold for a 5-gold vote, and stop chasing multipliers on buildings that do not score.
Ten things that took the community a launch week to work out. Every one traces back to a patch note,
a community guide or a named discussion thread — the sources page lists them all.
## 1. Placement value is not placement quality
The biggest preview number is not the best play, because it only shows this week. A building placed
where a range effect can reach several future placements beats one placed where it scores highest
right now. This is the point a launch-week guide video makes first, and it is the most common early
mistake.
## 2. The colour of the targeting line is a rule, not decoration
Light blue means adjacent; orange means range. Two cards can have near-identical text and want
completely different layouts. See the beginner guide.
## 3. Removing your own buildings is a tool
The entire Martial guild scores off removals — Guard Tower fires when something in range is removed,
Banner fires when the Banner itself goes. Agricultural has its own version: one player names
Composter plus farmland as their removal package. If you are treating demolition as damage control,
you are missing a scoring engine.
## 4. Trade 3 gold for 5 with the Merchant
With the Merchant councillor seated, buying a vote costs 3 gold and the vote gives 5. That loop is
how players clear the Spymaster’s 75-gold council request without building a run around it. Full
economy on the gold page.
## 5. Multipliers on non-scoring buildings are usually wasted
Two documented exceptions, and only two: there is a relic that applies mult to gold gain — which
makes mult on a Vineyard real — and adding the irrigator tag to a scoring building. Outside those,
the launch-week thread’s own conclusion was that it “seems pointless”.
## 6. Learn one blowup and aim for it
The most-cited one: two Marketplaces joined with rainbow paint, which a player describes as
triggering “x10 every turn for the rest of” the run. It needs gold to fish for the right paint,
which is a large part of why several players rate Commercial the strongest opener. More combinations
on the potions and paints page.
## 7. Paints run out
The launch-day patch reduced the durations of red, green, gold and blue paint. A paint is a timer, so
set up a paint-dependent loop early enough in the milestone that the rest of it can ride the loop.
## 8. You can end a milestone early — once you unlock it
A neutral unlock lets you close a milestone the moment you hit its target instead of playing out the
remaining weeks. It needs Neutral level 7 and a finished run. Knowing whether to bank early or keep
building is one of the decisions a launch-week guide video lists as under-explained.
## 9. Terrain decides which guild is good on this island
Maritime builds only on sea and shallow water. Mine needs rock adjacency. A long coastline is worth
more to one pair of guilds than a fat interior is, and maps have been procedurally generated since the
second demo. Details on the walls and terrain page.
## 10. If an achievement did not pop, restart the game
Guild unlocks resolve when a run ends, so in endless mode they can sit pending.
Patch v1.0.6 added a check on launch for exactly these, which makes closing and reopening the game the
documented fix rather than folklore.
## What is still unexplained
Two things nobody has answered, so do not lose time on them: what a railway connection actually does
for range effects, and whether anything can genuinely be placed on scaffolding. Both were raised in
launch week and neither got an authoritative answer. Version 1.0.5 added an in-game Compendium — the
developers’ own acknowledgement that the card text was doing too much work alone.

All 21 Combolands Guild Combos
Quick Answer
Combolands gives you 7 pickable guilds and every run takes 2 of them, so there are exactly
21 guild combinations — the same 21 the Guild Journeyman and Ultimate
 Guildmaster achievements ask you to win with. Only 0.8% of Steam owners have won once with all
 21, and 0.2% have done it on Regent. The secret guilds, Rogues and Arcane, are not part of the
 21 because they cannot be picked at the start of a run.
Every run picks 2 of the 7 base guilds, which is exactly 21 pairings — the number Crux Games quotes on the Steam page, and the number behind the Guild Journeyman achievement (win once with all 21) and Ultimate Guildmaster (win all 21 on Regent). Rogues and Arcane are unlocked separately and cannot be picked at the start, so they are not part of the 21. The 'how it reads' column is built from each guild's documented loop; the evidence column says whether a specific source discusses that pairing or whether we combined the two guild descriptions ourselves.

## Why 21 and not more
Seven guilds choosing two, order irrelevant, is 7 × 6 ÷ 2 = 21. Crux Games quotes the number on
 the Steam page and builds two achievements around it. Because Rogues
 and Arcane only enter a run partway through, adding them does not change the count — which is
 also why the completion achievements stayed at 21 after the secret guilds were in.
## Chasing Guild Journeyman
The two completion achievements are the rarest things in the game: Guild Journeyman
(win at least once with all 21 combinations) sits at 0.8% of owners and
Ultimate Guildmaster (the same 21 on Regent) at 0.2%. For scale, 5.4% of owners
 have beaten Regent once — so of the players who can clear the top rank, roughly one in twenty-seven
 has ground out every pairing at it. The achievements page has the
 full rarity ladder.
One practical warning from the launch week: guild achievements have been reported not popping in
endless mode until the game is restarted. Patch v1.0.6 added a check
 on launch for pending achievements, so restarting is the documented fix.

Combolands Guild Tier List (Community Vote)
Last updated: 2026-08-30
Two rankings, two different questions. This tier list is the community one: it tallies the best/worst votes in the launch-week Best & Worst Guild thread on Steam, where 15 players argued it out between 2026-08-28 and 2026-08-30. It is opinion, and small — read it as where experienced players were leaning in week one, not as solved theory. For the hard-numbers view, the ranks page and the guild pages carry the Steam global achievement rates instead.
## S Tier
| Name | Role | Why 
| Maritime | Self-sufficient scorer | Highest best-guild tally in the thread at 4.5 votes and zero worst votes. The recurring word is consistent: "a very consistent gameplan no matter what partner". Also the lowest victory-achievement rate of the seven at 19.7%, because it unlocks late. 
| Commercial | Gold engine | 3.5 best votes against 1 worst. Early gold via Gold Pan and Vineyard funds the shop, and the Marketplace loop is the single most-cited blowup: "2 marketplaces connected with rainbow paint on them will trigger x10 every turn". 
## A Tier
| Name | Role | Why 
| Martial | Enabler / demolish access | The most split guild after Frontier: 1.5 best and roughly 2.5 worst votes, but the defence is specific — "probably the strongest just for how good it is at enabling other buildings (and very consistent access to demolish)". Weak alone, strong as a partner. 
| Industrial | Multiplier ramp | 1 best vote, no worst votes. "One of the easiest to get massive multipliers going on". 
| Civic | Plot growth | No best or worst votes of its own, but named inside one player's top tier alongside Agriculture and Industry. Its Tax Collector reset is the mechanic to learn first. 
## B Tier
| Name | Role | Why 
| Agricultural | Enabler with transforms | 2 worst votes, no clean best votes — yet also the loudest defence in the thread: "I am constantly shocked at how low people see agriculture given how strong it is, can very easily win even without the 2nd guild with agriculture on Regent". Win rates are the share of all Steam owners holding that guild's victory achievement, pulled from Steam's public global achievement stats on 2026-08-30. Guilds unlock in sequence as you play, so this is a clear-rate order, not a power ranking — see the community vote on the tier list page for that.

| Agricultural | 73.9% | Crop and farm tags. Combolands Heirlooms: How They Work
Quick Answer
Heirlooms are Combolands' passive item slot — the Steam page promises
100+ passive heirlooms. Most sit in the background buffing your build,
 but a few only pay out when you sell them, and that is how the
Arcane guild is unlocked: fill the Jewellery Box with 8 gems,
 sell it for the Lost Crown, sell the Lost Crown for a Wizard vote.
## The rules that apply to all of them
Heirlooms are passive. The Steam description calls them "100+ passive heirlooms to further enhance your build", and the game treats them as a separate slot from buildings and blueprints.
Some heirlooms do something when you sell them rather than while you hold them — that is the whole basis of the Arcane guild unlock chain.
Since v1.0.6 (2026-08-28) your final gold score takes heirloom sell values into consideration, so unsold heirlooms are no longer dead weight at the end of a run.
Heirlooms arrive from heirloom chests, the shop and council request rewards. One of the potions added in the Next Fest demo makes the next heirloom chest contain only rare or better.
## Every heirloom named in a public source
Crux Games advertises 100+ passive heirlooms. Only a handful are named in public sources, and every one of those is named here with the source that names it. The rest are not listed because we have no way to check them — a table of invented heirloom effects would be worse than a short honest one.

## The Jewellery Box chain, step by step
Get the box. It can appear in the shop or in heirloom chests. The
Antiquarian councillor is the reliable route — it can hand you the
 box and then keep supplying gems.
Feed it 8 gems. Duplicates count; they do not have to be unique.
Sell the completed box to receive the Lost Crown.
Sell the Lost Crown to receive a Wizard vote, which unlocks Arcane.
If gems and tomes seem to vanish instead of entering the box or the Bookshelf, that was a real

Since v1.0.6 your final gold score takes heirloom sell values into consideration. Before that

 dumping your collection in the last week to convert it is no longer the obvious play.

Combolands Scoring, Triggers & Multipliers
Quick Answer
Combolands buildings come in two kinds. Some fire on their own every few weeks. Others say they
 do something "when triggered" — those need another building to fire them, and
 the game does not tell you which. Chaining the second kind is what separates the easy ranks
 from the rest: as one launch-week player put it, "to beat the game above easy you will need to
 use combos that don't rely on auto triggers usually".
## Reading a building's targeting
The clearest public explanation of the card layout comes from the Japanese community guide on the
 Steam hub. A building shows you its guild and rarity, its activation condition, its effect, the
 terrain it can sit on, and its tags. The effect draws a targeting line, and the colour of that
 line is the part people miss:
Light blue line = adjacent. Only the tiles touching it.
Orange line = range. A wider area around it.
Guard Tower "triggers when a building in range is removed"; Banner triggers when the
 Banner itself is removed. Same guild, completely different placement rules. The
buildings page lists which effects each documented building uses.
## Tags, not names
Most cross-building effects key off tags rather than specific buildings. Sunflower Field reads the
crop tag; Scarecrow reads farm; Ranch reads husbandry. Two buildings
 that look related in the art may share no tag at all, and two that look unrelated may chain
 perfectly.
## Transform then trigger is not transform back
A launch-week thread asked whether orchard fields and wheat farms were bugged because they seemed
 to change into something else and stay that way. The answer, from another player, was that this
 is exactly what the card says: "They don't say that they transform back. They say explicitly that
 they 'transform then trigger'. Sunflower says 'transform then trigger every 3 weeks'." Read the
 verb order literally — that is the whole rule.
## Base score, mult and xmult
Three layers stack, and they scale very differently. Endless-mode players describe pushing base
 score into the e11 range on Shrines while separately running thousands of mult and using Rogue
 Hideouts to drive an xmult on top. One player summarised the endgame priority as
 pivoting away from base score toward mult once enchantment was already compounding.
## When is a multiplier wasted?
Usually, on a building that does not score. A launch-week thread asked exactly this and got two
 concrete exceptions:
There is a relic that applies mult to gold gain, which makes mult on something like a
 Vineyard worth having. See heirlooms.
It matters "if you add the irrigator tag to scoring buildings" — the tag turns a non-scorer into
 part of a scoring chain.
Outside those, the thread's own conclusion was that it "seems pointless".
## Milestones and weeks
A run is a sequence of milestones, each with a score target and a number of weeks. The screenshots
 on the Steam page show the early ones — Small Town with a 3,000 target, Capital City with 13,000.
 Since the Next Fest demo there has been a neutral unlock that lets you end a milestone as soon as
 you hit its target rather than playing out the remaining weeks; it needs Neutral level 7 and a
 finished run to unlock. Timing that decision is one of the ten things a launch-week guide video
 lists as under-explained.
Past the named milestones, targets become Megalopolis I, II, III and so on — that is
endless mode, where the scaling maths becomes the whole game.
## Two things that still are not explained in game
 Railways. The card says anything connected to one part of the railway is
 connected to all of it. A player asked what that means for, say, connecting a building to a
 cluster of husbandry buildings, and no authoritative answer followed. Unconfirmed as of 2026-08-30.
 Scaffolding. Its text mentions a mult bonus for buildings placed on top of it,
 but the same player reports being unable to place anything on it. Also unconfirmed.
Version 1.0.5 added an in-game Compendium, which the developers describe as by far the most
 requested feature — a sign they know the wording is doing a lot of unassisted work.
Page re-checked: August 30, 2026

Combolands Secret Guilds: Rogues & Arcane
Quick Answer
Combolands has two secret guilds. Rogues unlocks by completing the Spymaster's
 council request — collect 75 gold during one milestone — then using the Spymaster vote it pays
 out. Arcane unlocks by filling the Jewellery Box heirloom with 8 gems, selling
 it for the Lost Crown, then selling the Lost Crown for a Wizard vote. Both stay unlocked for
 every future run, and neither can be picked at the start of one.
## Rogues
 Take the Spymaster's request. Complete the Spymaster's council request — collect 75 gold during a single milestone. The request pays out a Spymaster vote, and using that vote adds Rogues to your current run and every run after it. 
 Run Commercial to make the gold, Martial to re-fire it. The guide that
 documented this unlock recommends exactly that pairing: Commercial supplies buildings that
 grant gold, Martial supplies ways to trigger them again after placement. It singles out the
Ballista and the Gold Pan as the two pieces most likely to get your
 first real pile of gold together.
 Use the Merchant councillor's arithmetic. With the
Merchant in your council, buying a vote costs 3 while the vote
 gives 5. Repeating that is the cheapest documented way to clear a 75-gold target inside one
 milestone.
 Spend the vote. Using the Spymaster vote adds Rogues to your current run and
 to every run afterwards.
 What you get: Buildings that trigger other buildings and collect money, usually carrying a minor drawback. In endless runs the Rogue Hideouts are what players credit for runaway xmult. Cannot be picked at the start of a run. The guide author who documented the unlock calls it "the most powerful guild in this game". 
Source: Steam guide 'Guide: Unlocking Rogues & Arcane'; endless-mode thread
## Arcane
 Find the Jewellery Box. It can turn up in the shop or in heirloom chests —
 the guide's phrasing is "basically everywhere". The reliable route is the
Antiquarian councillor, which can both hand you the box and keep
 supplying gems if you keep feeding it votes.
 Collect 8 gems. They do not have to be unique, so duplicates count.
 Sell the completed box. That gives you the Lost Crown
heirloom.
 Sell the Lost Crown. That gives a Wizard vote, and using the Wizard vote
 unlocks Arcane.
 What you get: Buildings with unusual effects — strong, but by the same guide's reckoning not as strong as Rogues. Also not pickable at the start of a run. The Antiquarian councillor is the fastest route: keep feeding it votes and it can both hand you the box and keep supplying gems. 
Source: Steam guide 'Guide: Unlocking Rogues & Arcane'
## Two things that will make you think it failed
 New guilds unlock after the run ends, not at the moment you use the vote. If
 you are in endless mode, the guide's author notes the achievement
 only appears after you restart the game — and v1.0.6 added a launch-time check for exactly
 these pending achievements, which suggests the developers saw the same reports.
 Neither guild is selectable at the start of a run. They enter play through the
 run itself, so an empty guild-select screen is not a sign the unlock failed.

Combolands Walls, Terrain & Plateaus
Quick Answer
Walls in Combolands are about enclosure, not decoration — a wall line that does
 not fully close the area it is meant to close does nothing. That is the point of the launch-week
 community video titled "It's ALL ABOUT Walls in Combolands", and it is the terrain rule that
 most often costs people a milestone they thought they had.
## Terrain pieces and their placement counts
Placement counts are how many of each piece a single card gives you. All the numbers below come
 from Next Fest demo patch notes in April and June 2026 — the developers have not restated them
 since the 1.0 progression overhaul, so treat them as the last published values rather than as
 current gospel.
| Piece | Placements (last published) | What it does 
| Wall | 5 → 6 (demo v8) | The enclosure piece. Community guide coverage stresses that walls only pay off when the area they surround is properly closed — a gap is not a smaller bonus, it is no bonus. 
| Palisade | 7 → 9 (demo v8) | The cheaper, more plentiful enclosure piece — nearly twice as many placements as walls. 
| Plateau | 3 → 4 (demo v6), 4 → 5 (demo v8) | Raised terrain. Since v1.0.4 you cannot build plateaus on plateaus. Stockpile scores from Plateau correctly as of the same patch, and removing a Watch Tower creates trees on plateaus as of v1.0.6. 
| Railway / Rail | 2 → 3 (demo v6), 3 → 4 (demo v8) | Connection piece. Its card says anything connected to one part of the railway is connected to all of it; what that means for range effects has not been authoritatively answered. 
| Scaffolding | — | Its text mentions a mult bonus for buildings placed on top of it, but a launch-week player reports being unable to place anything on it. Unconfirmed. 
## The tile types
| Tile | Why it matters 
| Grass | The default buildable surface, and most of a generated island. 
| Sand | The beach ring. Citizens spawn on sand tiles as well as from Martial buildings, as of demo v9. 
| Rock | Mine must be built adjacent to rock, which is what makes the Industrial chain map-dependent. 
| Shallow water | The pale band around the island. Part of the Maritime guild’s buildable area. 
| Deep sea | The rest of the map. Also Maritime’s territory — every building in that guild is water-only. 
| Plateau | Raised ground you create. Cannot be stacked on itself since v1.0.4. 
## Why the island shape decides your run
Maps have been procedurally generated since the second demo, and demo v9 explicitly improved the
 generator "to make bigger islands". That makes terrain a constraint you draft around rather than
 a fixed layout you memorise:
 Maritime can only build on sea and shallow water, so an island with a
 long coastline is worth far more to it than a fat one.
 Industrial needs rock adjacency for Mine, so a rockless island cuts the
 front of its production chain.
Pairing Maritime with a land guild means the two halves of your build barely compete for tiles
 — one of the reasons players describe Maritime as consistent "no matter what partner".
## Removal is a terrain tool too
Because Martial scores off removals and gives consistent demolish access,
 knocking down your own buildings is a normal part of shaping the board rather than a mistake.
 Removing a Watch Tower even puts trees back on plateaus. The
scoring page covers which buildings pay you for that.
