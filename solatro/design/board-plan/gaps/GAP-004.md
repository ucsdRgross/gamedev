# GAP-004 — The palette has no white, and the match rim was ruled to be white
status: resolved
outcome: answered
raised: during S12 (the match highlight)
design: solatro/design/board-plan/DESIGN.md version 1, nodes F9, F11, F13; PLAN.md §1.10 (the owner
rulings during execution), NAMES.md (the `match_rim` / `match_rim_active` palette roles)
severity: CONTRADICTION
resolution: |
  Owner, verbatim: "a, gold for now but would realistically be a shader that causes color to slowly
  interpolate between different colors over time like a rainbow shimmer effect. you can implement
  that if its easy" and "i prefer blending for shimmer effect, samples would look too distracting with
  jumps."
  match_rim = 31, match_rim_active = 6 stand. The activated rim becomes a SHIMMER alert kind on the
  outline shader: the rim colour interpolates slowly over time through a ramp of palette entries -
  BLENDED, an owner-ruled exception to 4i's "ramps sample, they never lerp", stated at the shader.
  Step S23, by eye.

**What the design says** — PLAN §1.10, the owner's ruling during execution, verbatim: *"no outline on
mark when not being selected and on board, then white outline when indicating it matches current card
being selected to show it matches."* NAMES.md: *"`match_rim` — the outline an element takes while it
matches · `match_rim_active` — the realized form of `Q64`"*, and §4i's rule that every colour is a
palette POINTER with exactly one home.

**What the ANSWER says** — `Q64`, verbatim: *"activated art gets special outline, pips get their
outline replaced with a different version to indicate activated"*; `Q71`=(b): colour alone is
acceptable for these states. Neither names an entry, and the execution ruling names a colour
(*white*) rather than a role.

**What it does not say** — which palette entry each of the two roles points at.
`Assets/CircusCrayon.png` has 32 entries and **none of them is white**: the lightest is 31
`#eddcc0` (a warm cream, already `ball_gloss` — the juggled ball's specular dot) and the next is 19
`#cab9bc`. So the ruling's *white* is either (i) entry 31, this palette's stand-in for white, or
(ii) a request for a white entry that the palette does not yet carry. `match_rim_active` is
*"a different version"* and nothing narrows it at all.

**Why it blocks** — it is an owner call about the look (triage 2), and adding an entry to the shared
palette is owner-visible: it changes the image every role in the game indexes into. It is also a
design-versus-resource contradiction (triage 3): the ruling names a colour the palette cannot
resolve.

**Options I can see** —
**(a)** **`match_rim` = 31 `#eddcc0`** — this palette's white; the ink already used for the glare
band and the ball's specular, so it is the established "bright highlight" entry — consequence:
nothing changes in the image, and the rim reads as warm cream rather than pure white ·
**(b)** **add a white entry to `Assets/CircusCrayon.png`** and point `match_rim` at it —
consequence: a true white, at the cost of a palette image change and a re-check of every
palette-conformance snapshot ·
**(c)** **`match_rim` = 19 `#cab9bc`** — the palette's neutral light grey, cooler and closer to
white in hue but much darker — consequence: less emphatic against a dark cell ·
*my recommendation* (a).
On `match_rim_active`: **(i)** 6 `#f8c300`, gold — consequence: reads as "lit up" and is the only
bright warm entry not already spoken for as a suit colour · **(ii)** 2 `#e71b40`, red — consequence:
collides with the THROB alert colour and with `suit_fire` · **(iii)** 12 `#4fd7ff`, ice blue —
consequence: distinct from every rim in play, but reads as cold rather than as activation ·
*my recommendation* (i).

**Blast radius** — `Assets/Palette/roles.tres` (two entries), `Scripts/palette_roles.gd`
(two `@export`s, already registered in `ROLE_NAMES`); no code branches on either value, and
`Tests/UI/test_plan_visuals.gd` asserts the ROLE, never the number, so a ruling is a one-line
resource edit. Design nodes F9–F16 and S13's layer view, which reuses `match_rim_active`.

**Meanwhile** — parked: nothing. S12 shipped with the recommendation above ((a) + (i)) so the
mechanism could be built, tested and photographed; `Tests/Visual/plan_match_shot.tscn` renders
exactly what those two entries look like on the board, which is the picture the ruling wants to be
made against. A ruling of (b) or (c) moves one number in `roles.tres` and nothing else.
