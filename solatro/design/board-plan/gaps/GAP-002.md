# GAP-002 — When do the mark hooks fire, and where may a mark effect put its mult?
status: open
raised: during S6 (found by the Phase 1–3 adversarial review, before S9)
design: solatro/design/board-plan/DESIGN.md version 1, nodes D2, D3, D4, D7, C5, Q46, Q51, Q52, Q122
severity: GAP

**What the design says** — chart D: D2 *"a card lands on a marked cell"* → D3 *"on_mark_hit fires
when the landing card MATCHES"* / D4 *"a sibling hook fires for ANY cover, matching or not"* → D7
*"the effect keeps firing for as long as a card sits on the mark, every time a line through the cell
scores"*. PLAN §1.7: the two hooks are *"dispatched from `place_card_in_grid`"* (S9), and *"Fires
for as long as a card sits on the mark — every time a line through the cell scores (`Q52`=(b))"*.
Chart C5: *"sum every BONUS mult — a talent match, a hat match, and a mark effect that says x2
contributes +2"*.

**What the ANSWER says** — `Q46`=(d), verbatim: *"both hooks for now, but mark is a bad name for
this."* — promoted as *"both hooks ship — one for any cover, one for a match"*. `Q51`=(a): *"yes — the
×2 mark multiplies whatever is put on it, which is what makes it worth covering with anything"*.
`Q52`=(b): *"for as long as a card sits on it — every time a line through the cell scores"*.
`Q122`, owner: *"ideally two x2 should be a x4. +2 to mult, do not multiply 0 ever"*. None of them
says whether the hooks fire at LANDING as well as at every line score, and none names how an
effect hands its "+2" to the line — the hooks are `-> void`.

**What it does not say** — two decisions:
1. Do `on_mark_covered` / `on_mark_hit` fire at landing time (from `place_card_in_grid`, chart D's
   D2 read literally) AND at every line score through the cell, or ONLY when a line through the
   cell scores (`Q52`=(b) read as the one trigger, with D2 the moment the mark starts acting)?
2. A mark effect contributes its mult through `CardEffectApi.add_line_mult(amount)` (logged in
   ASSUMPTIONS.md at S6), which is only meaningful while `score_line` composes a line and asserts
   otherwise. If the hooks also fire at landing, the same effect asserts there. The reviewer also
   asks whether "every cover fires the cover hook, and a match fires the hit hook in addition"
   (the run's reading of `Q46`=(d)'s words) is what the owner meant, since chart D draws the two
   as an exclusive branch and the difference decides whether a cover effect and a hit effect both
   pay on one matching landing.

**Why it blocks** — two defensible choices differ in observable behaviour (a landing-time effect that
draws a card or reveals something would fire zero times under one reading), and shipped content
will be written against whichever API shape lands, which makes the choice expensive to reverse.

**Options I can see** —
**(a)** **score-time only** — the hooks fire from `score_line` for every meld card on a marked
cell, never from `place_card_in_grid`; `add_line_mult` keeps its precondition; a landing that
completes no line fires nothing — consequence: one dispatch site, no reconciliation; a cover
effect that wants to act on landing has no trigger ·
**(b)** **both, and `add_line_mult` outside a composition is ignored** — consequence: content can
act on landing; a mult reported from the landing dispatch is silently dropped, the shape that hides
bugs ·
**(c)** **both, and the hook carries the context** — the `level` argument (or a third value) says
"landing" versus "scoring", so an effect adds its mult only when scoring — consequence: content can
tell, at the cost of a wider hook contract than NAMES.md fixes ·
*my recommendation* (a): it is the reading under which `Q52`=(b), chart C5 and the composition seam
all hold without a second mechanism.
On the cover/hit question: **(i)** every cover fires `on_mark_covered`, a match ALSO fires
`on_mark_hit` (the run's current reading of the answer's words) · **(ii)** exclusive, as chart D
draws it · *my recommendation* (i).

**Blast radius** — plan steps S9 (TP-46, TP-47, TP-48, TP-49, TP-50, TP-51); design nodes D2–D7;
`NAMES.md` hook signatures if (c).
