# GAP-006 — the ellipse's aspect is fixed but its SIZE is not, so no ring has a radius
status: resolved
outcome: answered (superseded by GAP-009)
raised: 2026-08-15, during execution plan step S4 (`WallPacker`)
design: DESIGN.md version 5, nodes G1, G2, G4
severity: GAP

## Owner's answer — option (a) is REJECTED

> "ring radii isnt hardcoded. why would it be be when picture frames can be different sizes.
> Find best fit instead."

**Radii are derived from the actual picture sizes, never authored and never a literal.** The
reasoning is decisive and kills option (a): frame thickness is per-picture (`frame_px` on
`PictureEntry`, §1.1) and `size_multiplier` is free, so any hand-tuned radius would need retuning
every time art changed. A derived radius is also the only reading that satisfies §1.8's no-literals
rule without adding fields.

This confirms P11 falls out for free rather than needing a special case: best fit for a single
picture is radius 0, which centres it — exactly `Q19`=(b) / P11's "one rect, centred".

⚠ **One sub-question is still open, and it decides whether four planned tests exist.** "Best fit"
does not by itself say what happens to the 7th picture when 6 fit:

- **(i) the ring OVERFLOWS** — each ring's radius is set by the extent of the ring inside it plus
  `gap_px`, so a ring has a real circumference and a real capacity; extras spill to the next ring.
  Keeps `Q11`=(b), `G2`, and tests **P2, P3, P4, P12** exactly as written. Nothing is authored:
  every number comes from `design_size`, `size_multiplier`, `frame_px` and `gap_px`.
- **(ii) the ring GROWS** — the radius expands until everything assigned to it fits, so a ring
  always fits its contents. Simpler and tighter-looking, but capacity becomes vacuous: no ring can
  ever overflow, so `Q11`=(b), `G2`, **P2, P3** describe behaviour that cannot occur and must be
  withdrawn.

Both are "best fit" and neither is hardcoded. **(i) is the reading that preserves the plan**, and is
assumed unless the owner says otherwise; S4 stays parked until confirmed, since building (ii) and
discovering it was (i) means rewriting the packer's core and four of its tests.

### Owner's answer — (i) OVERFLOW, and the ring abstraction itself is rejected

> "overflow is correct, grow implies gap between rings increasing which is bad. I think the framing
> of it as rings is actually bad, since a large frame could theoretically overlap multiple rings. Its
> more like continuously placing around a center point at angles in a star pattern but not literally
> a star pattern"

**Overflow is confirmed**, and the reason given is sharper than the one in this gap: growing a ring's
radius pushes the *next* ring further out, so the inter-ring gap silently inflates. That is a
consequence neither option above named.

⚠ **The second half supersedes this gap rather than closing it.** Discrete rings are rejected as the
model: a picture large enough spans what would be several ring-bands radially, so a single shared
radius per ring cannot describe it. Placement is instead continuous around a centre point by angle,
with each picture's distance from centre falling out of best fit.

This invalidates more than S4. `PictureEntry.ring` is an authored field already shipped in **S3**;
§1.3's rules 3 and 4 are written in ring terms; and **P2, P3 and P4 assert ring membership**. The
concrete decisions the new model needs are filed as **GAP-009**. S4 stays parked on that, not on
this. This gap is answered.


**What the design says** — `G1`: *"Ellipse aspect follows the live window aspect, clamped to an
authored min and max. Q10=c"* (`DESIGN.md:631`). `G2`: *"Ring capacity is computed — as many as fit
given picture sizes and the gap. Q11=b"* (`DESIGN.md:632`). `PLAN.md` §1.3 rule 3 states it
operationally: *"fill a ring until the next picture's outer width plus `gap_px` would exceed the
ring's circumference"* (`PLAN.md:109-110`).

**What the ANSWER says** — `Q10`=(c): *"it equals the window aspect, clamped to an authored min/max
so an ultrawide window does not produce a pancake"* (`DESIGN.md:899`). This fixes a **ratio** and
nothing else. `Q11`=(b): *"as many as fit given picture sizes and the separation gap, computed"*
(`DESIGN.md:900`) — this presupposes a circumference to fill. `Q14`=(a) fixes the gap as one uniform
number (`DESIGN.md:903`). `Q5` governs wall-view fit/fill framing (`DESIGN.md:891`), and `PLAN.md`
§1.2 annotates `view_margin` as the camera's *"crop bias"* — a framing parameter, not ring geometry.
None of these settle the question: an aspect ratio plus a gap plus picture sizes still admits
infinitely many ellipse scales.

**What it does not say** — **what fixes a ring's radius.** `WallPacker.pack(layout, unlocked,
window_aspect)` (`PLAN.md` §1.3) has no input that determines absolute ring scale: `WallLayout`
(§1.2) carries `gap_px`, `home_id`, the two ellipse-aspect clamps and `view_margin`, and no radius,
ring-spacing or wall-extent field; `PictureEntry` (§1.1) carries `ring` and `slot` indices but no
radius. Picture sizes and `gap_px` are absolute wall units, so the pictures have a real size while
the ellipse they sit on has none.

⚠ **The circularity is the crux, and it is why this cannot be answered locally.** If a ring's radius
is *derived from its contents* (grow until the members fit), then capacity is vacuous — a ring
always fits exactly what is in it, no ring ever overflows, and `Q11`=(b), `G2` and tests **P2** and
**P3** describe behaviour that cannot occur. If the radius is *fixed first*, capacity is computable
exactly as written, but nothing in any of the four documents fixes it. One of these must give.

**Why it blocks** — triage test (2): two defensible choices differ in observable behaviour — the
same layout produces visibly different walls, and under the derived reading the overflow-to-next-ring
behaviour disappears entirely. It is also expensive to reverse: ring geometry is the packer's core,
`TestWallPacker`'s fixtures are written against it, and `S10`, `S36` and the `S34` tool all consume
it. **`TEST_PLAN.md` P2 cannot be written at all** — its fixture requires *"ring 0 circumference
fitting exactly 6"*, which cannot be constructed without knowing what sets that circumference.

⚠ It is additionally an owner call because `PLAN.md` §1.8 rules that **no number here may be a
literal**: *"If you find yourself typing a literal into a `.gd` file that is not in one of those
three places, stop — that is a gap."* Any radius formula's constants would be exactly such literals,
so the answer must land as authored fields, and `S34` must expose them — its done-when requires
*"every `WallLayout` field"* be live-editable.

**Options I can see** — **(a)** ring radii are **authored** on `WallLayout` — a base radius plus a
per-ring step, or an explicit per-ring list — so capacity stays computed per `Q11`=(b) exactly as
written, P2's fixture becomes constructible by setting the radius, and `S34` exposes the numbers like
every other tunable; the wall no longer self-sizes to its contents and an author must retune the
radius when picture sizes change · **(b)** ring radii are **derived from the ring's contents** —
each ring grows until its members plus gaps fit exactly — so the wall always looks tight with no
authored number to maintain; this makes capacity vacuous and **contradicts** `Q11`=(b), `G2`, P2 and
P3, which would have to be withdrawn · **(c)** ring radii derive from a **fixed schedule off the
home picture** — ring 0 from the home picture's size, ring *N* = ring *N−1* + the tallest picture in
ring *N−1* + `gap_px` — so no new authored field is needed and capacity stays computable, at the cost
of coupling ring spacing to content and leaving the schedule's own constants unauthored ·
*my recommendation* **(a)**, because it is the only option that satisfies §1.8's no-literals rule and
S34's "every `WallLayout` field" requirement without withdrawing planned tests
