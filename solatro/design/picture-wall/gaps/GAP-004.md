# GAP-004 — the DPI the 9 mm touch target is derived from is not trustworthy
status: resolved
outcome: answered
raised: 2026-08-15, during the post-chart engine verification pass
design: DESIGN.md version 3, nodes I8
severity: GAP
resolution: |
  Answered (b) in chat: keep 9 mm from `DisplayServer.screen_get_dpi()` as `Q123`=(b) said, but
  CLAMP the result to a sane pixel range so a bad EDID, a second monitor inheriting the primary
  screen's DPI, or Android's averaged `DisplayMetrics` cannot produce an absurd control.

  The clamp is a contract, not a guard clause: it needs a floor and a ceiling in the tunables table
  (`wall_touch_target_min_px`, `wall_touch_target_max_px`), and the clamped value is what every
  touch control uses.


**What the design says** — `Q123`=(b), charted inside **I8**: *"Targets are 9 mm physical from
reported DPI."*

**What the ANSWER says** — `Q123`=(b) *"9 mm physical, derived from the reported DPI"*, no note. The
goal — a target that is the same real-world size on every device — is right; the input to the
calculation is the problem.

**What the docs and issues say** — `DisplayServer.screen_get_dpi()` is unreliable in exactly the
cases that matter. Reported DPI depends on monitor EDID data that is frequently wrong, which is why
Godot has a separate `screen_get_scale()` that asks the OS for its display scale factor instead
([godot-proposals#2661](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/2661)). On Windows
with multiple monitors, DPI is taken from the **primary** screen and propagated to all of them
([godot#110106](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/110106)). On Android the true DPI cannot
be obtained at all because of permission restrictions; the value returned is an average of
`DisplayMetrics` x and y
([godot-proposals#10811](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/10811)).

**What it does not say** — what to do instead. `screen_get_scale()` is the recommended replacement
for hiDPI detection, but it answers "how much is the OS scaling" rather than "how big is a
millimetre", so it is not a drop-in for a physical-size rule.

**Why it blocks** — it is a `⚑contract` number that goes straight into the plan. Writing "9 mm from
`screen_get_dpi()`" hands an implementer a formula that silently produces the wrong size on a
second monitor and on most Android devices, with nothing failing.

**Options I can see** — **(a)** a fixed target size in design-resolution pixels, the 44 px `Q123`(a) offered, with no DPI involved — predictable everywhere, wrong-sized on a genuinely unusual display · **(b)** 9 mm from `screen_get_dpi()` as you answered, but clamped to a sane pixel range so a bad EDID or a second monitor cannot produce an absurd control · **(c)** derive from `screen_get_scale()` with a per-platform fallback constant, accepting that it answers scale rather than physical size · *my recommendation* (b) — it keeps your intent and the clamp is what makes it safe

**Blast radius** — design node I8; the tunables table.
